Financial Statement Analysis

A practical guide to analysing financial statements, identifying financial strengths and risks, and turning accounting information into useful financial insights.

FINANCIAL ANALYSIS

What is financial statement analysis?

Financial statement analysis is the process of examining financial statements to understand a company's financial performance, financial position and cash-generating ability.

Analysts use information from the income statement, balance sheet and cash flow statement, together with financial ratios and comparative information, to evaluate the business.

Financial statement analysis can help students, investors, managers and finance professionals identify trends, strengths, weaknesses, risks and opportunities.

THE THREE CORE STATEMENTS

Financial statements you should understand

INCOME STATEMENT

Profitability and Performance

The income statement shows revenue, expenses and profit over a specific accounting period.

It helps analysts assess revenue growth, margins, operating costs and profitability.

BALANCE SHEET

Financial Position

The balance sheet presents assets, liabilities and equity at a particular point in time.

It helps assess liquidity, leverage, capital structure and financial stability.

CASH FLOW STATEMENT

Cash Generation

The cash flow statement explains movements in cash from operating, investing and financing activities.

It helps determine whether a business is generating sufficient cash to support its operations and investments.

ANALYSIS FRAMEWORK

How to analyse financial statements

01

Examine the financial statements

Start by understanding revenue, expenses, assets, liabilities, equity and cash flows.

02

Compare financial performance

Compare current results with previous periods, budgets, targets or relevant competitors.

03

Calculate financial ratios

Use liquidity, profitability, solvency and efficiency ratios to identify important relationships in the numbers.

04

Identify trends and risks

Look for improving or declining margins, increasing debt, liquidity pressure, weak cash generation and other signals.

05

Interpret the results

Explain what the numbers mean rather than simply listing calculations.

06

Reach a conclusion

Summarise the company's financial strengths, weaknesses, risks and areas requiring further investigation.

KEY ANALYSIS AREAS

What should you look for?

A strong financial statement analysis connects the financial numbers to business performance.

PROFITABILITY

Is the business generating profit?

Examine revenue growth, gross profit, operating profit, net profit and profitability margins.

LIQUIDITY

Can the business meet short-term obligations?

Analyse current assets, current liabilities and liquidity ratios to assess short-term financial capacity.

SOLVENCY

Is the capital structure sustainable?

Examine debt levels, equity financing and the company's ability to manage long-term obligations.

EFFICIENCY

How effectively are resources being used?

Examine asset utilisation, inventory management, receivables and other operating relationships.

RATIO ANALYSIS

Connect financial statements with ratios

Financial ratios transform financial statement figures into relationships that can be compared over time or against appropriate benchmarks.

Liquidity Ratios

Current ratio and quick ratio can help assess short-term financial obligations.

Profitability Ratios

Net profit margin, return on assets and return on equity can help evaluate profitability.

Solvency Ratios

Debt-to-equity and other leverage measures can help assess long-term financial risk.
