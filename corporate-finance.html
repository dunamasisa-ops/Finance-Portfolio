CORPORATE FINANCE

What is corporate finance?

Corporate finance focuses on how businesses make financial decisions. It considers how companies invest capital, obtain financing and manage financial resources.

Corporate finance decisions can affect profitability, risk, liquidity, growth and the long-term value of a business.

The major areas include investment decisions, financing decisions and working capital management.

CORE CORPORATE FINANCE TOPICS

The major areas of corporate finance

These concepts form the foundation of corporate financial decision-making.

INVESTMENT DECISIONS

Capital Budgeting

Capital budgeting evaluates long-term investment opportunities and helps determine whether projects are financially worthwhile.

FINANCING DECISIONS

Capital Structure

Capital structure considers the mix of debt and equity used to finance a company's activities and investments.

COST OF CAPITAL

Required Return

The cost of capital represents the return required by providers of debt and equity capital.

SHORT-TERM FINANCE

Working Capital

Working capital management focuses on current assets, current liabilities, liquidity and short-term operations.

FINANCIAL MANAGEMENT

Risk and Return

Corporate finance involves balancing expected returns against financial and business risks.

FINANCIAL STRATEGY

Long-Term Decisions

Financial strategy connects investment, financing and operating decisions with the broader objectives of a business.

CAPITAL BUDGETING

Evaluating investment opportunities

Capital budgeting helps businesses evaluate projects that require significant investment over multiple periods.

01

Net Present Value

NPV compares the present value of expected project cash inflows with the initial investment.

02

Internal Rate of Return

IRR is the discount rate at which the net present value of a project equals zero.

03

Payback Period

Payback measures the time required for a project to recover its initial investment from its cash inflows.

04

Profitability and Risk

Investment decisions should consider expected returns, cash flows, risk and relevant assumptions.

COST OF CAPITAL

Understanding the cost of financing

Businesses use different sources of finance, and each source has an associated cost or required return.

DEBT

Cost of Debt

Represents the effective cost associated with borrowing and debt financing.

EQUITY

Cost of Equity

Represents the return required by equity investors for providing capital to the business.

COMBINED FINANCE

WACC

Weighted Average Cost of Capital combines relevant financing costs according to their capital weights.

CAPITAL STRUCTURE

Debt, equity and financial leverage

Capital structure refers to the combination of debt and equity financing used by a business.

Financial leverage can affect both potential returns and financial risk. The appropriate financing mix depends on the company's circumstances and strategy.

WORKING CAPITAL MANAGEMENT

Managing short-term financial resources

Working capital management focuses on maintaining an appropriate balance between liquidity and operational efficiency.

RECEIVABLES

Accounts Receivable

Businesses need to manage customer credit and collections to support cash flow.

INVENTORY

Inventory Management

Efficient inventory management can help reduce unnecessary working capital requirements.

PAYABLES

Accounts Payable

Managing supplier payment terms can affect both liquidity and relationships with suppliers.
